# Vertical Slice: The Empty Granary

## Purpose

The vertical slice tests one hypothesis:

> Can a simulated disruption create a journey the player understands, cares
> about, resolves through several play styles, and later recognizes through a
> changed world?

It is not intended to demonstrate every planned feature.

## Starting region

The slice uses:

- Three kingdoms
- Eight to twelve provinces
- Five to seven settlements
- One agricultural exporter
- One mining settlement
- One crossroads market
- One fortress controlling a bridge
- One legal carriage route
- One smuggling route
- One bandit faction
- One subterranean monster colony
- One persistent mine-dungeon
- Ten to fifteen named characters

## Causal chain

1. Seasonal drought reduces the agricultural settlement's food production.
2. Regional food reserves fall and prices begin rising within bounded limits.
3. A neighbouring kingdom schedules an official relief shipment.
4. A disputed fortress closes the bridge and demands military inspection.
5. The shipment is delayed while the mining settlement consumes its reserves.
6. Displaced workers and deserters reinforce a bandit group near an alternate
   surface road.
7. Mine owners open a sealed lower tunnel to bypass the bridge and maintain
   production.
8. Excavation disturbs a subterranean colony that begins damaging roads and
   shafts.
9. Competing factions attempt to control the food, route, and public account of
   the crisis.

The player initially witnesses empty market stalls, a guarded granary, and a
known family unable to buy bread—not a statistics screen.

## Interested actors

The exact identities are generated, but the slice requires these roles:

- Agricultural supplier seeking payment and safe delivery
- Fortress commander defending the closure
- Mining guild leader protecting production
- Worker or refugee representative seeking immediate food
- Bandit leader with displaced followers
- Smuggler offering an illegal route
- Scholar or hunter who understands the subterranean colony
- Local official whose legitimacy depends on the outcome

Each actor controls a material resource, institution, route, or body of people.

## Intervention A: official convoy

The player accepts the legal shipment and attempts to cross the contested
bridge.

Possible play includes:

- Securing permits or political backing
- Accepting inspection and delay
- Bribing or exposing the commander
- Defending the convoy from displaced bandits
- Sacrificing cargo space for guards or refugees

Likely consequences:

- Legal market receives food
- Government legitimacy improves
- Military or merchant faction gains leverage
- Bandit recruitment pressure may remain
- Dungeon and monster pressure continue unresolved

## Intervention B: reopen the bridge

The player addresses the bottleneck rather than carrying the first shipment.

Possible play includes:

- Repairing damage
- Investigating whether the closure is legitimate
- Negotiating between kingdoms
- Removing a military or monster threat
- Exposing deliberate obstruction

Likely consequences:

- Several shipments can move instead of one
- Route capacity and future trade improve
- The responsible faction loses or gains power
- The crisis resolves more slowly but more structurally
- Smuggling becomes less profitable

## Intervention C: dungeon smuggling route

The player cooperates with miners or smugglers to move food through the sealed
lower route.

Possible play includes:

- Preparing an underground expedition
- Negotiating with the bandit faction for access
- Containing, relocating, or fighting the subterranean colony
- Choosing between food, refugees, tools, and recovered relics
- Deciding who controls the route afterward

Likely consequences:

- Food arrives quickly but illegally
- Black-market and bandit influence may grow
- Mine production may recover
- Monster ecology changes
- The dungeon may become a public route, criminal passage, sealed reserve, or
  faction-controlled asset

## Intervention D: exploit or refuse

The player may buy scarce food, carry luxury cargo instead, serve the faction
benefiting from the shortage, or leave the region.

The world should continue coherently:

- Prices rise
- Population displacement grows
- Bandit recruitment increases
- Government legitimacy falls
- Emergency actors pursue imperfect alternatives
- The player later meets people and places changed by refusal

Refusal is a supported decision, not a failure to accept content.

## Required local spaces

- One city map with state-dependent dressing
- Coaching inn and depot
- Market and guarded granary
- Faction or government office
- Black-market access point
- Bridge checkpoint segment
- Bandit-controlled route segment
- Mine entrance and persistent dungeon
- Subterranean colony habitat

## Required immediate projections

At least four channels change after intervention:

- Market stock and price
- Street population and behaviour
- Traffic and shipment movement
- Security and inspections
- Faction presence
- Dialogue and services
- Physical route or dungeon state

## Required delayed echoes

Within thirty to ninety simulated days, the player receives at least two:

- Letter from a known character
- Returning passenger
- Changed faction demand
- New or closed route
- Altered monster pressure
- Successor bandit group or legitimized settlement
- Repurposed dungeon space
- Visible growth, recovery, or decline on return

## Slice completion condition

The slice is complete only when all primary interventions:

- Are playable from initial witness to delayed echo
- Produce deterministic and measurably different strategic outcomes
- Change named characters and physical places
- Support combat and at least one non-combat resolution
- Preserve save/load equivalence
- Pass the validation gates in [Validation](09-validation.md)
