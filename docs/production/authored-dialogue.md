# Authored Dialogue

## Voice target

Characters speak clearly, but not flatly. The setting enters through their
work, loyalties, habits, and ordinary vocabulary. A miner talks about shifts,
wooden supports, blocked tunnels, and lamps. A clerk talks about counts,
delivery papers, receipts, and named destinations. A smuggler talks about tax
patrols, cover, timing, and who can be trusted.

Most lines should sound natural when read aloud. The vivid part comes from a
precise observation, a concealed motive, or the speaker's sense of humour.
One exact detail is worth more than a decorative metaphor.

## Scene rule

Every line needs these answers before it is written:

1. Who is speaking?
2. What do they want the player to do now?
3. What do they know first-hand?
4. What do they avoid saying?
5. Which words come from their work or social position?

If another character could say the line unchanged, the line needs another
pass.

## First-use clarity

Every spoken line must make sense without a glossary or quest log. Name an
unfamiliar object by what it does before using its local name. State the plain
fact before adding a joke, threat, or strange detail.

Do not use one word for different objects in the same scene. For example,
`seal` could mean a blocked tunnel, wax on a letter, or an official stamp.
Write `bricked-up tunnel`, `unopened letter`, or `receipt` instead.

A setting name can add flavour after the line establishes the basic action.
`Take the food past the tax patrol` works on first reading. A secret name such
as `foxfire supper` does not, unless the scene has already explained it.

## Dialogue and interface

Dialogue carries motive, opinion, incomplete knowledge, and pressure. The
interface carries exact quantities, prices, deadlines, controls, and verified
objectives. A character may repeat a number when bargaining, but does not need
to recite the whole quest card.

Player choices are short spoken intentions. The wording must make the action
clear before it is selected. The resulting simulation command remains the
authority.

## Patterns to remove

- Vague agents such as `something below` when the speaker can report a sound,
  mark, missing person, or damaged object.
- Unfounded personification such as a lying mine, a remembering road, or
  flour that refuses to answer. The dragon's hoard is the exception because
  the simulation establishes its memory as a literal fact.
- Polished oppositions such as `not this, but that` when a person would simply
  name the problem.
- Closing aphorisms such as `both things are true`.
- Lines that explain the theme instead of pursuing a goal.
- Fantasy synonyms added only to make a sentence sound old.
- Unexplained work jargon, secret names, and local titles on their first use.

## Revision pass

Read the line aloud in the scene. Cut any opening that repeats visible context.
Replace abstract nouns with the person, tool, place, or decision involved.
Check that the speaker is asking, warning, bargaining, refusing, hiding, or
reporting—not presenting a summary for the player.

The catalogue test rejects several known vague or personified constructions
and keeps each spoken line within the conversation panel's 96-character limit.

## Research basis

- Failbetter's published writer guidelines favour short, observable prose,
  direct speech, clear actions, and restraint with period style:
  <https://www.failbettergames.com/news/fallen-london-writer-guidelines-part-iii>
- BioWare describes owning and standardising individual character voices,
  reading every line aloud, and moving background information out of dialogue
  when it would make a character sound like a tour guide:
  <https://blog.bioware.com/2022/09/29/bioware-community-update-writing-our-worlds/>
- Ryan M. James's GDC talk on realistic performances argues that functional,
  systemic lines still need the personality of the person saying them:
  <https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024641/Realistic-Performances-in>
- Evan Skolnick's GDC dialogue session focuses on removing verbal padding,
  adding emotion, and making each word earn its place:
  <https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1013171/-em-And-Make-It>
