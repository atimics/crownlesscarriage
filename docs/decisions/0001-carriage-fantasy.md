# 0001 — The carriage company is the central player fantasy

**Status:** Accepted

## Context

The earlier concept described the player as an envoy, merchant, investigator,
and mercenary. That collection of roles provided many verbs but no strong
identity and risked turning the player into a universal procedural errand-runner.

## Decision

The player operates the last politically independent carriage line under a
cross-border charter. Transporting people, goods, information, and contraband
is the common interface between political economy and character-scale adventure.

## Consequences

- The carriage becomes the home, party, inventory, progression, and travel
  interface.
- Capacity and time force meaningful trade-offs.
- Contracts, dungeons, monsters, bandits, politics, and trade must connect to
  movement across the network.
- Features unrelated to the carriage fantasy require stronger justification.

## Rejected alternatives

- Playing a ruler would overemphasize strategy and reduce character-scale travel.
- A generic adventurer would lack a unique relationship to the simulation.
- A pure merchant would narrow political and dungeon participation too far.
